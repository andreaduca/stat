Independence, tested

Contingency tables, χ², and association vs causality · Statistics · MSc Cybersecurity, Sapienza · Homework 7 · November 2026

Abstract

Every earlier homework assumed independence — of the bits of a seed, of the steps of a walk, of the samples of a mean. This one tests it. For two categorical variables the procedure is short: count the table, compare it with the independent table that has the same totals, measure the gap with χ², and read three things — the evidence (p), the strength (Cramér's V, bits) and what the result does not mean. On Berkeley's 1973 admissions the pooled gap between men and women is overwhelming (p = 7.8 × 10⁻²²) and disappears inside the departments (p = 0.22): Simpson's paradox. On random generators the test catches a textbook generator at once, and passes another one that is still fully predictable. On a masked chip it finds a leak that the standard test of averages cannot see. Throughout, association is not cause, and “not significant” is not “independent”.

Each claim is labelled with its evidence: theorem proved in the notes or on the theory page · measured on these pages · source a published result (checked 18 Nov 2026) · opinion the author's view.

1 · Introduction

Independence is the quiet assumption under most of statistics and of cryptography. The law of large numbers and the central limit theorem of HW4 and HW5 add up variances, which needs independent draws; the entropy of a wallet seed in HW2 counts its bits as independent coin flips; the secrecy of a cipher in HW3 is, by Shannon's definition, the independence of ciphertext and message. When the assumption fails, two different things go wrong theorem: a secret leaks, because an observation that depends on it tells something about it; and error bars lie, because correlated draws carry less information than they seem — with a correlation of 0.5 between consecutive draws a “95 %” interval covers the truth only 74 % of the time (theory). This homework turns the assumption into a question that data can answer.

2 · Theory

In probability, X and Y are independent when P(X = x, Y = y) = P(X = x)\,P(Y = y) for every pair of values, or equivalently when P(Y = y \mid X = x) = P(Y = y): knowing X changes nothing about Y. It is a property of a model, true or false. In statistics we only see counts O_{ij} from a sample, and their exact twin of independence — every row of the table having the same profile, or O_{ij} = R_i C_j / N in every cell — almost never holds even for truly independent variables theorem. Two groups of 100 with the same true rate differ by up to ±13.6 points 95 % of the time. So the question becomes: is the gap larger than chance would make it?

The expected count E_{ij} = R_i C_j / N, the row total times the overall share of the column, is not a recipe but the maximum-likelihood fit of the independence model: the independent table closest to the data theorem. Once the totals are fixed only (r - 1)(c - 1) cells are free; that count is the degrees of freedom. The statistic \chi^2 = \sum (O_{ij} - E_{ij})^2 / E_{ij} adds up each gap in units of the size chance gives it (a count expected to be E wanders by about \sqrt E); under independence it follows the χ² law with those degrees of freedom — exactly for two categories, where it is the square of a standardised binomial theorem, and asymptotically in general (Pearson 1900, Fisher 1922 source). Two traps mark the limits of weaker notions: Y = X^2 with X uniform on {−1, 0, 1} has zero correlation and total dependence, and the XOR of two independent bits is independent of each of them but fixed by the pair.

3 · The procedure, on Berkeley

In 1973 the six largest departments of UC Berkeley received 4 526 applications; 44.5 % of the men and 30.4 % of the women were admitted (Bickel et al. 1975 source). Following the five steps of the procedure page measured: (1) the 2 × 2 table of gender × admission; (2) its row profiles, 44.5 % vs 30.4 %; (3) the expected table — if gender did not matter, 2 691 × 1 755 / 4 526 = 1 043.5 men would have been admitted, against 1 198; (4) the gap, χ² = 92.2 on 1 degree of freedom; (5) the decision, p = 7.8 × 10⁻²², far below α = 0.05, with a weak strength, V = 0.14. Steps 1–4 are exact integer arithmetic; only the p-value needs numerics, computed in logarithms so that even 10^{-14875} remains a number. The table is a sufficient statistic: counts from different sources simply add up, and the raw records can be discarded — the method runs on any CSV in the browser without the file leaving the device.

4 · Results

Random generators

A generator is random when its next output cannot be guessed from the current one. On 10 000 pairs of consecutive 4-bit outputs measured, the last 4 bits of a textbook linear congruential generator (LCG: x_{n+1} = (a\,x_n + c) \ \text{mod}\ 2^{32} with a = 1664525, c = 1013904223) fill only 8 of the 256 cells of the pair table: χ² = 70 000, V = 1. The reason is a theorem: those bits follow their own 16-state generator, y \mapsto 13y + 15 \ \text{mod}\ 16, and repeat every 16 outputs. A generator that repeats its output 5 % of the time is caught with p = 3 × 10⁻³⁸ (detected for good after about 900 pairs; 80 % detection is promised at 2 015 by the power formula and reached 77 % of the time in simulation). xoshiro128** and the LCG's first 4 bits pass (p = 0.90 and 0.84). Yet the LCG remains fully predictable: 8 outputs carry its whole 32-bit state. Coldcard hardware wallets (2021–2026) made the same point at a cost of over a thousand bitcoin: a small deterministic generator whose output passed the tests and whose seeds could be enumerated (HW2 source). A test of independence catches visible patterns; it cannot certify unpredictability.

Berkeley 1973

Inside the departments the gap disappears measured: women are admitted more often in four of the six, and the only department with a significant difference, A, favours women (82 % vs 62 %, p = 3 × 10⁻⁵). Combining the six departments without mixing them (the Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test) gives p = 0.22 and a common odds ratio of 0.90. The pooled rate of each gender is a weighted average of the department rates, with that gender's own weights; men applied mostly to departments A and B, which admit most applicants, and women mostly to C–F, which admit few. Simpson's paradox needs exactly these two associations — gender × department (V = 0.49) and department × admission (V = 0.41) — and cannot happen if the weights are the same theorem.

Any dataset

The same five steps run on a file of the reader's choice, with categories made from numbers by bins fixed in advance measured. Fixing the bins first matters: trying bin after bin until p drops below α is a hidden multiple test.

5 · Interpretation: evidence, strength, power

A p-value mixes two things, the strength of an association and the amount of data. Adding Berkeley's records one chunk at a time separates them measured: V settles near 0.14 while χ² keeps growing, roughly in proportion to N, and stays significant from about 360 records on. With enough data every real association, however weak, becomes significant, so the strength must be reported next to p. The converse matters as much: with 4 526 records the test finds an association of strength V ≥ 0.042 80 % of the time; a weaker one could be there and go unnoticed. “Not significant” means no evidence at this sample size, never “independent”. A test can contradict independence; it cannot confirm it opinion, after Popper.

6 · Association vs causality

A χ² test measures P(Y \mid X), what is seen together; a causal claim is about P(Y \mid do(X)), what would happen if X were set. In a simulated world where old devices have both an old operating system and more infections, but the OS does nothing, the infection rates differ 23.2 % vs 8.0 %; setting the OS by a coin flip gives 14.6 % vs 15.1 %, and comparing inside device groups then averaging gives 15.0 % vs 13.9 % measured (causality). Three structures produce association without cause: a common cause, a mediator (whose effect is real but vanishes inside its groups, exactly like a common cause in the tables), and a selection — two independent alarms become strongly dependent (φ = −0.70) once only the cases where one fired are kept. The table alone cannot tell these apart; the causal diagram, a claim about the world, can theorem. For Berkeley, the pooled table answers “were women admitted less often?” (yes, through where they applied) and the departmental one “did departments treat equal applicants differently?” (no evidence); which question matters is not a statistical choice opinion.

Base rates turn association into error at scale. An alarm that catches 99 % of targets and clears 99 % of innocents, looking for 1 target in 100 000, raises about 10 010 alarms per million people, of which 10 are real theorem (Bayes; Axelsson 2000 on intrusion detection source). Being flagged is strongly associated with being a target, and still almost every flag is wrong. Reading a group-level association as a fact about the person flagged is where statistics turns into harm opinion.

7 · Security connections

An observation O reveals nothing about a secret S exactly when S \perp O, that is when the mutual information I(S; O) = H(S) - H(S \mid O) is zero theorem: leakage is dependence, measured in bits. Encrypting 200 000 letters of a skewed text with a fixed shift (Caesar) leaves the ciphertext with 3.93 bits about each letter, all of its entropy; a fresh random key per letter (the one-time pad) leaves 0.002 bits, the level of pure noise measured (mutual information). That noise level matters: measured information is biased upward, and independent 4 × 4 tables of 100 pairs show 0.068 bits on average.

On a simulated chip computing one step of the PRESENT cipher, the power is the number of 1 bits of an intermediate value plus noise measured (side channels). Masking splits the value into two random shares; every class is then centred on the same power, but its variance is 4 - \text{HW}(v) theorem. Welch's t-test, the averages-only check of the standard TVLA methodology (Goodwill et al. 2011 source), is blind to it: it detects the leak in 0.5 % of 200 repeats with 10 000 traces, the false-alarm rate. The χ² test of independence, which compares whole distributions, detects it in all 200, from about 500 traces (as proposed by Moradi et al. 2018 source). Masking moves the leak to a higher moment; it does not remove it.

8 · Nature

The contingency table was born in heredity (nature). Mendel's peas — 315, 108, 101 and 32 seeds in the four combinations of shape and colour — give χ² = 0.12, p = 0.73: independent assortment. Bateson, Saunders and Punnett's sweet peas do not (V = 0.70); the dependence, explained by Morgan as genes on the same chromosome, became in Sturtevant's hands the first genetic map source. Fisher later found Mendel's totals too good: χ² = 41.6 on 84 degrees of freedom, a fit that honest chance beats only about 3 times in 100 000 — the lower tail of χ² flags data too regular to be true. And Snow's cholera study of 1854 shows how a causal question can be settled without an experiment: two water companies supplied the same London streets almost at random, and the houses drinking water drawn below the sewers had 8.5 times the deaths source.

9 · Final considerations

The procedure is short and general, and the hard part is reading it opinion. Three readings carry over to any table. First, p, V and power answer different questions — could this be chance, how strong is it, could the test have missed it — and a result needs all three. Second, association is not cause; whether to compare inside groups depends on a causal diagram that the data do not reveal. Third, in security a test of independence is a leak detector, valuable precisely because a leak is a dependence; but it sees only the dependence it looks at, so passing it is evidence, never a proof of safety. The generators that pass and are still predictable, the masked chip that passes the classic test and still leaks, and the wallets whose seeds passed every test are the same lesson three times.
